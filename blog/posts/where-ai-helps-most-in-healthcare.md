title: Where I Think AI Helps Most in Healthcare
date: 2026-06-27
tags: [Healthcare, NLP, Responsible AI]
---

Healthcare is full of AI hype, and a lot of it deserves skepticism. But there are places where the technology genuinely earns its keep — usually where it removes toil rather than replaces judgment.

Here's where I'm most optimistic.

## 1. Turning unstructured notes into structured signal

A huge amount of clinical information lives in free text — notes, discharge summaries, referrals. NLP that can reliably extract entities and relationships (without inventing them) frees clinicians from data-entry and makes downstream analytics possible.

## 2. Reducing administrative burden

Prior authorizations, coding, documentation — this is exactly the kind of high-volume, rule-heavy work that automation handles well. Every hour a model saves on paperwork is an hour returned to patients.

## 3. Surfacing, not deciding

The best healthcare AI I've seen *surfaces* things for a human to act on — a flagged anomaly, a summarized history, a ranked worklist — and leaves the decision with a clinician.

## The guardrails aren't optional

If AI touches health data, three things are non-negotiable:

- **Privacy by design** — de-identification and access control aren't features you add later.
- **Bias auditing** — a model that works on one population and fails on another is worse than no model.
- **Monitoring** — performance drifts, and in healthcare drift has consequences.

> The goal isn't autonomous medicine. It's giving clinicians better tools and more time.

That's the kind of AI I want to build.
